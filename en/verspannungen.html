Measures Against Muscle Tension

There are many different approaches to easing muscle tension — from a quick stretch in between tasks to professional treatment. The overview below groups the most common measures into categories and briefly explains what each one is meant to achieve. None of this is a promise of a cure: for persistent or severe symptoms, seeing a doctor is always a good idea.

Movement & Stretching

Targets shortened or overloaded muscles directly and restores range of motion in the short term.

🙆

Neck stretch (lateral, diagonal)

Stretches the side neck muscles, which often shorten from holding your head at an angle toward the screen.

🔄

Shoulder rolls / shoulder blade squeezes

Mobilizes the shoulder joints and activates the muscles between the shoulder blades, which barely get used while sitting.

🚪

Chest-opener stretch (e.g. in a doorway)

Stretches the front chest muscles, which shorten when the shoulders are pulled forward, countering the typical rounded-shoulder posture.

🐈

Cat-cow (spinal mobilization)

Moves the spine alternately into flexion and extension, aiming to loosen the back muscles.

🖥️

Micro-exercises right at the desk

Brief neck and shoulder movements you can fit in without leaving your seat — exactly the approach PostureShift supports with its reminders.

🧘

Yoga (targeted for neck/back)

Combines stretching, strengthening, and conscious breathing, covering several angles at once.

Heat

Promotes blood flow to the muscles and is experienced by many as noticeably relaxing.

🌡️

Hot water bottle / cherry pit pillow

A simple heat source you can target locally on the neck, shoulder, or lower back.

🔌

Electric heating pad

Delivers longer-lasting, adjustable heat — practical for extended use in the evening.

🛁

Warm bath / warm shower

Works broadly across the whole body and combines well with stretching exercises afterward.

♨️

Sauna

A similar effect to a warm bath, plus a general relaxing effect from alternating heat and cooling.

🩹

Heat patches

Delivers steady, low-level heat directly to the affected spot for several hours — even under clothing during the day.

Manual Self-Help & Tools

Applies targeted pressure to tense muscle areas without needing a second person.

🧻

Foam roller

Uses your own body weight to apply broad pressure to muscles and fascia, for example along the back.

⚫

Massage ball / peanut ball

Allows more targeted pressure than a foam roller — suited to smaller, hard-to-reach spots like between the shoulder blades.

🔫

Massage gun

Produces fast, pulsing bursts of pressure on the muscles and is often used for short-term loosening before or after exertion.

🟢

Acupressure mat

Many small pressure points spread across a larger surface, typically used by lying your back on it for a few minutes.

🎯

Trigger point self-treatment

Targeted, sustained pressure on individual, often painful muscle knots — usually applied with a ball or your own fingers.

⚡

TENS device

Electrical muscle stimulation through skin electrodes, which some experience as pain-relieving and relaxing.

Professional Treatment

Draws on expertise and techniques that are difficult or impossible to apply on your own.

🏥

Physiotherapy

Combines targeted exercises, manual techniques, and guidance, tailored individually to the specific complaint.

💆

Massage therapy

Releases tense muscles through targeted manual techniques and promotes local blood flow.

🦴

Osteopathy

A holistic manual approach that considers restricted movement anywhere in the body as a possible cause of muscle tension.

🧑‍⚕️

Chiropractic

Focuses on the mobility of the spine and adjacent joints.

📍

Acupuncture

Places fine needles at defined points on the body, rooted in traditional Chinese medicine.

🫙

Cupping

Uses suction cups to create a local pull on the skin, intended to stimulate blood flow to the tissue beneath.

🩺

Medical evaluation

Worthwhile for persistent, recurring, or severe symptoms, to rule out other causes and identify the right treatment.

Effectiveness and the strength of the evidence vary considerably by method — some of the approaches listed here are well studied scientifically, while others are considered controversial by parts of the expert community. This list only explains what each method is meant to achieve; it does not evaluate how well it works.

Ergonomics at the Workplace

Addresses the cause instead of treating symptoms after the fact.

🪑

Height-adjustable desk

Lets you switch between sitting and standing, creating different loading patterns over the course of the day.

💺

Ergonomic chair / backrest

Supports the natural curve of the spine and reduces one-sided strain while sitting.

🖥️

Monitor height & distance

Avoids a constantly forward- or downward-tilted head position, which strains the neck muscles.

⌨️

Keyboard and mouse ergonomics

Reduces one-sided strain on the wrist, forearm, and shoulder during long screen work.

💪

Armrests

Relieve the neck and upper back when set correctly — more on that on the Relieving Arm Strain page.

Without strength training, the effect is only short-lived. The measures listed above usually relieve acute tension only temporarily. If the affected muscles aren't sufficiently trained for the demands of daily life — for instance because hours of sitting barely engage them — renewed tension is likely to follow soon after. Stretching, heat, massage, or relaxation techniques provide short-term relief but don't replace building strength in the muscle groups involved. A combination tends to work best in the long run: the short-term measures from this list plus targeted, regular exercise for the muscles that everyday life neglects.

It's understandable that time is often the limiting factor — extra training sounds like one more commitment in an already full day. In practice, though, this impression often reverses: once you experience the pleasant effect of a more stable, resilient body, you spend noticeably less time dealing with pain, tension breaks, and their treatment — and end up reclaiming time in your day as a result.

Relaxation & the Mental Side

Muscle tension often has a psychological component as well as a physical one — stress frequently comes with elevated muscle tension.

🌊

Progressive muscle relaxation (PMR)

Deliberately alternates between tensing and relaxing individual muscle groups to build awareness of baseline tension.

💨

Breathing exercises

Calm, deliberate breathing has a relaxing effect via the nervous system and can release unconsciously raised shoulders.

🧠

Meditation / mindfulness

Builds awareness of tension in the body, often a prerequisite for consciously releasing it at all.

🛌

Autogenic training

A self-relaxation technique using internally repeated phrases meant to induce muscle relaxation and calm.

🧩

General stress reduction

Addresses the root cause of elevated baseline tension instead of just treating its muscular consequences.

Daily Life & Lifestyle

Background conditions that lay the groundwork for less tension in everyday life.

😴

Enough sleep / an ergonomic pillow

Good sleep supports muscle recovery; the right pillow avoids extra nighttime strain from poor neck posture.

💧

Drinking enough water

Adequate fluid intake is often cited as a basic requirement for normal muscle function.

🥗

Magnesium

Often discussed in connection with muscle function; if you suspect a deficiency, it's worth getting checked by a doctor or nutrition professional rather than guessing on your own.

🔁

Regular position changes

Prevents sustained one-sided strain from building up in the first place, instead of treating tension after it has developed — the core approach behind PostureShift.
